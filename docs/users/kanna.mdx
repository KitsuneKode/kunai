A **kanna** (鉋) is a Japanese hand plane. You run it over rough wood and the roughness leaves in one curl.

Kunai is the blade. Kanna is who holds it.

## She is deliberately brief

Kanna works nights. She is not a viewing companion and she will not watch with you.

That restraint is the point, not a limitation. Kunai's whole promise is getting out of your way, and a mascot that chatters in your terminal contradicts the product it belongs to. So she is never excited; her range runs from calm, to focused, to mildly put-out. A full session from launch to quit shows at most four lines from her.

If she ever feels like she is talking too much, that is a bug. [Open an issue](https://github.com/KitsuneKode/kunai/issues).

## Where she shows up

She appears during the waits and on the failures, because those are the moments a terminal would otherwise leave you looking at nothing: the provider race, a search that came back empty, a resolve that did not work, the setup wizard from [Getting Started](/docs/users/getting-started), and the goodbye. She is never there for a success, and never while you are reading a list or watching something. Once playback is running she is out of the frame.

The longest wait in Kunai is finding a stream, so that is where she works hardest. It is one short story in four beats:

```text
❀  Resolving via AllAnime
   ◓ Providers
   🦊 a few providers deep.          she leans in while they race

   ◑ Stream                          one beat: the stream is found and carried

   ◒ Player
   🦊·over to the player.            she sits back; the player has it

── or ───────────────────────────
×  Could not resolve a stream
   🦊 hit a wall on that one.
```

On a quick resolve the last two beats are over before you can read them, and that is fine: they exist for the slow ones, when the player takes a while to come up.

If a provider dies and the race moves on she says so (`that one's a dead end.`), if the wait runs long she says that instead (`this one's being slow about it.`), and a stream found only after one of those is the one time she remarks on finding it (`got one, and it plays.`).

On an empty list or an error she adds one line under the message:

```text
·  No results for "dune 2099"
   Try a different spelling, or switch catalog.
   🦊 empty for now.
```

## What she says

Every line is one short sentence, lower case, and never excited. Each belongs to a moment, and each moment is something the program actually did, never a timer of her own:

| Moment                | Sounds like                                 |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| a search goes out     | `on it.`                                    |
| providers racing      | `checking which mirrors are awake.`         |
| one provider died     | `mirror's down, on to the next.`            |
| it is taking long     | `taking its time.`                          |
| found, the hard way   | `got one, and it plays.`                    |
| handing to the player | `over to the player.`                       |
| resuming              | `picking up where you left off.`            |
| first run             | `let's get you set up.`                     |
| no network            | `no network, but your library still works.` |
| a list is empty       | `nothing here yet.`                         |
| something failed      | `couldn't get there.`                       |
| quitting              | `go rest.`                                  |

A session that goes launch, search, resolve, play, quit hears four of them.

## Poses

Each pose maps to something real that is happening, so she never becomes decoration.

| Pose    | Reads as                        | In the terminal                                         | On this site           |
| ------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| `idle`  | sitting, ears up, unimpressed   | the setup summary                                       | home, social cards     |
| `wait`  | square and composed             | the setup wizard                                        | empty shelves, install |
| `seek`  | pitched forward, ears laid back | providers racing, rerouting, or dragging                | not yet                |
| `go`    | trotting, a dart in her mouth   | a stream found and being carried to the player          | searching, resolving   |
| `watch` | sitting back, eyes half-lidded  | the player coming up with the stream                    | docs hub               |
| `oops`  | ears flat, brows angled         | a resolve or a search that failed                       | 404s                   |
| `nap`   | curled, eyes closed             | a frame nothing has been asked of, and the goodbye      | the roamer asleep      |
| `peek`  | head and paws over an edge      | a search that found nothing: she looks over the message | not yet                |

All eight are one fox, traced from one model sheet into one layered drawing.

## Three ways she can appear, and none is required

The text is the tier that matters. The picture is what a capable terminal gets on top.

| Your terminal                         | What you get                                                   |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Kitty, Ghostty                        | the illustrated fox, and she moves: a blink, one ear, a breath |
| tmux, passing through to one of those | the same, when `allow-passthrough` is on                       |
| iTerm2, WezTerm, a Sixel terminal     | the illustrated fox, still                                     |
| everything else                       | `🦊` and her line, with a one-cell flick beside it             |

On Kitty and Ghostty the movement belongs to the terminal. Her drawings are sent once and the terminal plays them on its own clock, so a blink costs Kunai nothing and never repaints your screen. She is sized from your terminal's real cell size, so she is drawn one pixel to one and stays sharp.

She needs room. On a short terminal (under about 28 rows in a list, 30 while loading) and wherever a poster is on screen, the picture steps aside and the line stays. Which terminals qualify for images on your system is covered in [Platforms](/docs/users/platforms).

Half-block rendering is deliberately skipped. This art turns to noise at two pixels per cell, and a smear is worse than a glyph.

## Turning her off, and back on

Inside the app, `/pet` toggles her and remembers it: hide her once and she stays hidden on the next launch, bring her back once and she stays. The same switch lives in **Settings → General → Mascot & tips** (config key `companionPet`, `auto` or `off`). While she is hidden, the footer keeps a `/pet kanna` hint so the way back is never a secret.

```text
/ pet  →  Kanna is resting. /pet brings her back.
/ pet  →  Kanna is back.
```

The environment variable still exists and still wins for the run it is set on. It is the per-invocation pin, not the stored preference:

| Setting           | Effect                                                                 |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| unset             | the picture where your terminal can draw it, the glyph everywhere else |
| `KUNAI_PET=glyph` | keep the companion, but only ever as `🦊`                              |
| `KUNAI_PET=off`   | retire her entirely; no picture, no glyph, no lines                    |

```bash
KUNAI_PET=off kunai
```

An explicit `KUNAI_PET` outranks the stored preference: `/pet` will say so rather than pretend it changed anything, and the footer hint stays hidden because the toggle genuinely cannot work under a pin. `KUNAI_PET=off` silences the text too, not just the artwork. Redirected output (a pipe, a redirect, a captured log) never gets either, so nothing decorative can end up in a file or break a parser.

`KUNAI_POSTER=0` turns off images without retiring her, since the glyph is not an image. Both variables sit alongside the rest of the environment overrides in [Customization](/docs/users/customization).

## Reduced motion

Every animation on this site and in the terminal is gated on a reduced-motion preference: your system's on the site, and `KUNAI_REDUCED_MOTION=1` or `NO_MOTION=1` in the terminal. With it on she holds still everywhere. In the terminal that is literal: one drawing is sent and no animation frames follow, the line appears whole instead of typing on, and the cell beside her glyph stays empty.

The roaming fox on this site is the exception: she is not frozen, she is not rendered at all. Something that follows your pointer has no still version worth showing, so `KunaiFoxRoamer` returns nothing under reduced motion and the stylesheet hides her as a second guard. Every other surface keeps its artwork; only the movement stops.

Plain mode (`--plain`, `KUNAI_PLAIN=1`) retires her completely, the same as `KUNAI_PET=off`.
