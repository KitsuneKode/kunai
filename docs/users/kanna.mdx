A **kanna** (鉋) is a Japanese hand plane. You run it over rough wood and the roughness leaves in one curl.

Kunai is the blade. Kanna is who holds it.

## She is deliberately brief

Kanna works nights. She is not a viewing companion and she will not watch with you.

That restraint is the point, not a limitation. Kunai's whole promise is getting out of your way, and a mascot that chatters in your terminal contradicts the product it belongs to. So she is never excited; her range runs from calm, to focused, to mildly put-out. A full session from launch to quit shows at most four lines from her.

If she ever feels like she is talking too much, that is a bug. [Open an issue](https://github.com/KitsuneKode/kunai/issues).

## Where she shows up

She appears while you wait and when something fails, and on the surfaces that would otherwise leave you looking at nothing: an empty frame, a search that found nothing, the setup wizard from [Getting Started](/docs/users/getting-started), and the goodbye screen. She is never drawn on success, and never on a surface you are reading — results, details, settings, or a running episode with its poster on screen.

```text
⠋  Resolving via VidLink
   [seek]  checking which mirrors are awake.
```

```text
·  No results for "dune 2099"
   Try a different spelling, or switch catalog.
   🦊 empty for now.
```

```text
×  Could not resolve a stream
   3 providers tried · allanime, miruro, vidlink
   🦊 couldn't get there.
```

Each line fires when something changes, never on a timer. A healthy session — search, resolve, hand off, quit — meets at most four of them.

## Poses

Each pose maps to something real that is happening, so she never becomes decoration.

| Pose    | Reads as                          | Terminal                                                       | Site                     |
| ------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| `seek`  | leaning in, ears back, eyes wide  | a search in flight, the provider race, a provider dying        | searching, resolving     |
| `go`    | mid-stride, the dart in her mouth | the beat between "found it" and the player taking over         | home flow, install pages |
| `oops`  | ears flat, brows angled           | a failed resolve, a search that errored                        | —                        |
| `peek`  | paws over the edge, looking       | a search that came back empty                                  | 404s, section rules      |
| `nap`   | curled, eyes closed               | a bare frame nothing has been asked of yet, the goodbye screen | the roamer asleep        |
| `wait`  | square and composed               | the setup wizard                                               | empty shelves, install   |
| `idle`  | sitting, ears up, eyes soft       | the setup summary                                              | home, social cards       |
| `watch` | knowing, one ear tipped           | playback, when there is no poster to show                      | docs hub                 |

All eight are drawn from one layered vector, and every one has a moment that draws it.

## Three tiers, and only one of them is a picture

**The text tier is the one that matters.** The illustrated fox needs a terminal that can host a graphics protocol, which is a minority of runs, so the character has to hold up with the picture switched off. Which terminals qualify on your system is covered in [Platforms](/docs/users/platforms).

| Terminal                                           | What you get                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| kitty, Ghostty (and tmux with `allow-passthrough`) | her, animated by the terminal itself: she blinks, flicks an ear, and leans in while providers race |
| iTerm2, WezTerm, Sixel terminals                   | her as a still                                                                                     |
| everything else, SSH included                      | `🦊` and her line                                                                                  |
| `KUNAI_PET=off`, plain mode, redirected output     | nothing at all                                                                                     |

On kitty and Ghostty she costs one upload per pose. The terminal keeps the drawings and plays them by itself, so typing, scrolling and resizing never redraw her, and she is deleted from the terminal the moment she leaves the screen and again when Kunai exits, even on Ctrl+C. `NO_COLOR` drops the picture and keeps the line.

Half-block rendering is deliberately skipped. This art turns to noise at two pixels per cell, and a smear is worse than a glyph.

## Turning her off, and back on

Inside the app, `/pet` toggles her and remembers it: hide her once and she stays hidden on the next launch, bring her back once and she stays. The same switch lives in **Settings → General → Mascot & tips** (config key `companionPet`, `auto` or `off`). While she is hidden, the footer keeps a `/pet kanna` hint so the way back is never a secret.

```text
/ pet  →  Kanna is resting. /pet brings her back.
/ pet  →  Kanna is back.
```

The environment variable still exists and still wins for the run it is set on. It is the per-invocation pin, not the stored preference:

| Setting           | Effect                                                                 |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| unset             | the picture where your terminal can draw it, the glyph everywhere else |
| `KUNAI_PET=glyph` | keep the companion, but only ever as `🦊`                              |
| `KUNAI_PET=off`   | retire her entirely; no picture, no glyph, no lines                    |

```bash
KUNAI_PET=off kunai
```

An explicit `KUNAI_PET` outranks the stored preference: `/pet` will say so rather than pretend it changed anything, and the footer hint stays hidden because the toggle genuinely cannot work under a pin. `KUNAI_PET=off` silences the text too, not just the artwork. Redirected output (a pipe, a redirect, a captured log) never gets either, so nothing decorative can end up in a file or break a parser.

`KUNAI_POSTER=0` turns off images without retiring her, since the glyph is not an image. Both variables sit alongside the rest of the environment overrides in [Customization](/docs/users/customization).

## Reduced motion

Every animation on this site and in the terminal is gated on your reduced-motion preference — the system setting on the site, `KUNAI_REDUCED_MOTION` or `NO_MOTION` in the terminal, and plain mode. With it on, the terminal is sent one drawing and no animation frames, and her line appears whole instead of typing on. She does not breathe, lean, or react.

The roaming fox is the exception: she is not frozen, she is not rendered at all. Something that follows your pointer has no still version worth showing, so `KunaiFoxRoamer` returns nothing under reduced motion and the stylesheet hides her as a second guard. Every other surface keeps its artwork; only the movement stops.
